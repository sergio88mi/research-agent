## Themes across the evidence

- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and neural-retrieval architectures are empirically shown to ground LLM responses, reduce knowledge hallucination, and improve long-term agent memory. [2, 6, 9, 10, 11, 14, 20, 22, 24, 27]
- Advanced memory management, context window optimization, and structured memory retention mechanisms (such as DSMR and ACE) help maintain contextual consistency and reduce error rates in autonomous agents. [12, 16, 17, 18, 19]
- Self-deliberation, verification frameworks like COVE, and reflection modules (such as DiLu) provide structured execution steps to minimize errors and enable continuous evolution. [3, 28]
- Multi-agent debate and cross-examination frameworks (such as MAD and LM vs LM) successfully leverage interacting agents and sparse communication topologies to encourage divergent thinking and capture factual errors. [30, 31, 33, 34]

## Gaps

- Sub-question 3 lacks direct empirical studies evaluating general internal self-correction mechanisms outside of specific verification methods (like COVE) and reflection modules (like DiLu), as many survey papers do not report novel empirical tests for execution error minimization.
- Sub-question 1 contains multiple broad theoretical surveys and systematic reviews that discuss RAG taxonomy rather than presenting direct empirical tests of retrieval techniques specifically tailored to autonomous agent hallucination reduction.
