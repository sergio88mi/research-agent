# Research brief

**Question:** What approaches reduce hallucination in LLM-based autonomous agents?

**Interpretation:** This research explores the methodological, architectural, and evaluation strategies employed to mitigate factual errors and fabrications in autonomous agents powered by large language models.

## Sub-questions

1. What prompt engineering and reasoning frameworks minimize hallucination in LLM agents?  
   *query:* `large language model agents hallucination reduction prompting reasoning`
2. How do external knowledge retrieval and tool integration affect agent reliability?  
   *query:* `retrieval augmented generation tool use agent factuality verification`
3. What self-correction and memory management mechanisms are used to catch errors during agent execution?  
   *query:* `autonomous agents self correction memory management hallucination mitigation`
4. How are hallucination rates and factual accuracy evaluated in multi-step agent workflows?  
   *query:* `factuality evaluation hallucination measurement multi step llm agent workflows`
   *reformulated once from:* `evaluation benchmarks hallucination detection multi step language model agents`

## Evidence notes (one per approved paper, from its abstract)

- [1] Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (2022) - This paper investigates aligning language models with user intent and reducing untruthful outputs by fine-tuning GPT-3 using supervised learning on demonstrations and reinforcement learning from human feedback. The resulting InstructGPT models demonstrate improvements in truthfulness and reductions in toxic output generation compared to GPT-3. This bears on the sub-question by showing that fine-tuning with human feedback improves model alignment and truthfulness.
- [2] A Survey of Large Language Models (2026) - This paper provides a systematic survey of recent advancements in large language models across pre-training, post-training, utilization strategies, and evaluation methods. It reviews utilization strategies such as in-context learning, prompt engineering, and agentic reasoning that optimize deployment. This relates to the sub-question by highlighting prompt engineering and agentic reasoning as utilization frameworks for LLMs.
- [3] Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Models (2024) - This paper studies the ability of language models to deliberate on their responses to correct mistakes and develops the Chain-of-Verification (COVE) method, where a model drafts a response, plans and answers verification questions independently, and generates a final verified response. The experiments show that COVE decreases hallucinations across various tasks. This bears directly on the sub-question by introducing a reasoning framework that minimizes hallucination.
- [4] Siren's Song in the AI Ocean: A Survey on Hallucination in Large Language Models (2023) - This paper surveys recent efforts on the detection, explanation, and mitigation of hallucinations in large language models, presenting taxonomies and evaluation benchmarks. It analyzes existing approaches aiming at mitigating hallucinations and discusses future research directions. This relates to the sub-question by providing an overview of how hallucinations are addressed in the broader literature.
- [5] Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models (2023) - This paper introduces FunSearch, an evolutionary procedure pairing a pretrained LLM with a systematic evaluator to discover new mathematical and algorithmic constructions and avoid confabulations or hallucinations. It demonstrates that this approach surpasses best-known results in the cap set problem and online bin packing. This bears on the sub-question by presenting a framework that pairs LLMs with systematic evaluation to mitigate incorrect statements.
- [6] 🧜Siren’s Song in the AI Ocean: A Survey on Hallucination in Large Language Models (2025) - This article surveys recent efforts on the detection, explanation, and mitigation of hallucinations in large language models, addressing challenges regarding content that diverges from user input, contradicts context, or misaligns with world knowledge. It presents taxonomies of hallucination phenomena and analyzes mitigation approaches. This relates to the sub-question by providing a survey context on LLM hallucinations.
- [7] Retrieval Augmentation Reduces Hallucination in Conversation (2021) - This paper explores the use of neural-retrieval-in-the-loop architectures for knowledge-grounded dialogue to maximize knowledgeability and conversational ability. This bears on the sub-question by demonstrating an architectural approach that reduces hallucination in conversational models.

It evaluates baseline prompts and guideline-based prompts on two clinical tasks. This relates to the sub-question by showing how task-specific prompt engineering can improve model performance and reliability.
- [9] Survey and analysis of hallucinations in large language models: attribution to prompting strategies or model behavior (2025) - This paper presents a survey and empirical analysis of hallucination attribution in LLMs, introducing a framework with metrics for Prompt Sensitivity and Model Variability to determine whether hallucinations stem from prompt optimization or model behavior. This bears on the sub-question by analyzing how prompting strategies contribute to or mitigate hallucinations.
- [10] Detecting and Preventing Hallucinations in Large Vision Language Models (2024) - This paper introduces M-HalDetect, a multimodal hallucination detection dataset for fine-grained annotations on visual question answering examples, and optimizes InstructBLIP through Fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization to prevent hallucinations. - [11] Leveraging Generative AI and Large Language Models: A Comprehensive Roadmap for Healthcare Integration (2023) - This study conducts a scoping literature review to address the need for guidance on integrating generative AI and large language models (LLMs) into healthcare and medical practices.
