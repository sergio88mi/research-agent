# Research brief

**Question:** How effective is telemedicine for managing type 2 diabetes in adults?

**Interpretation:** This research evaluates the clinical and practical efficacy of remote digital healthcare interventions in treating and monitoring adult patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus.

## Sub-questions

   *query:* `telemedicine telehealth type 2 diabetes adults glycemic control HbA1c outcomes`

   *query:* `remote patient monitoring type 2 diabetes medication adherence self management adults`
3. What are the patient and provider barriers, facilitators, and overall acceptance regarding the use of telemedicine for diabetes care?  
   *query:* `telehealth telemedicine type 2 diabetes patient acceptance barriers facilitators adults`

   *query:* `telemedicine telehealth cost effectiveness economic evaluation type 2 diabetes adults`

## Evidence notes (one per approved paper, from its abstract)

- [1] Do Mobile Phone Applications Improve Glycemic Control (HbA1c) in the Self-management of Diabetes? A Systematic Review, Meta-analysis, and GRADE of 14 Randomized Trials (2016) - This systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 randomized trials investigated the effect of mobile phone applications on glycemic control in diabetes self-management. It reports that all type 2 diabetes studies showed a reduction in HbA1c, with a mean reduction of 0.49% for participants using an app compared to controls. This bears on the sub-question by demonstrating that mobile-app interventions can effectively lower HbA1c levels in adults with type 2 diabetes.
- [2] Computer-based diabetes self-management interventions for adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (2013) - This paper is a systematic review protocol and overview aimed at assessing the effects of computer-based diabetes self-management interventions on health status and quality of life for adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus. The abstract describes the background, objectives, and search methods without reporting final results. Consequently, it bears on the sub-question only by outlining the framework to evaluate such computer-based programs.
- [3] The impact of telehealth remote patient monitoring on glycemic control in type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis of systematic reviews of randomised controlled trials (2018) - This study conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of systematic reviews of randomized controlled trials to evaluate the effectiveness of telehealth on glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes. Pooling four systematic reviews, the authors report that telehealth interventions produced a small but significant improvement in monitoring HbA1c levels. This directly addresses the sub-question by confirming that telehealth positively impacts glycemic control.
- [4] Telehealth Interventions to Support Self-Management of Long-Term Conditions: A Systematic Metareview of Diabetes, Heart Failure, Asthma, Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease, and Cancer (2017) - This paper performed a systematic metareview of randomized controlled trials examining telehealth interventions to support self-management across six long-term conditions, including diabetes types 1 and 2. The objective was to assess the impact of telehealth on disease control and healthcare utilization and to identify effective components of support. Because the abstract details only the methods and objectives without stating specific findings, its bearing on the sub-question is limited to its evaluative framework.
- [5] Effect of telemedicine on glycated hemoglobin in diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials (2016) - This systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials summarized the effectiveness of various telemedicine methods compared with usual care for managing diabetes in adults. The authors report that telemedicine achieved significant but modest reductions in HbA1c across all three assessed follow-up periods, with a mean difference of -0.57% at 3 months or less. This directly supports the sub-question by showing that telemedicine improves glycemic control in diabetes patients.
- [6] Comparative effectiveness of telemedicine strategies on type 2 diabetes management: A systematic review and network meta-analysis (2017) - This systematic review and network meta-analysis investigated the impact of different telemedicine strategies on glycemic control in type 2 diabetes patients based on randomized controlled studies. Over a median 6-month follow-up, telemedicine reduced HbA1c by a mean of 0.43%, and network analysis showed that most strategies (except telecase-management and telementoring) significantly reduced HbA1c compared to usual care. This bears on the sub-question by demonstrating the specific HbA1c-lowering efficacy of various telemedicine approaches.
- [7] Telemedicine for the Management of Glycemic Control and Clinical Outcomes of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Studies (2017) - This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized evidence on the effectiveness of telemedicine interventions for managing glycemic and clinical outcomes in type 1 diabetes patients. ## Themes across the evidence

- Telemedicine and mobile health interventions consistently demonstrate a positive impact on glycemic control, showing significant reductions in HbA1c levels for adults with type 2 diabetes compared to usual care. [1, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13]
- Patient and provider adoption of telemedicine is influenced by various external environmental and infrastructural factors, internal user behaviors and motivations, and theoretical acceptance predictors such as perceived usefulness. [20, 21, 22, 23, 25, 27, 28, 29]
- While some literature evaluates economic evaluations and potential cost savings or time savings associated with telehealth and digital health programs, direct conclusive evidence on their cost-effectiveness compared to traditional in-person care remains limited or scarce. [31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36]

## Gaps

- Sub-question 2, regarding how remote patient monitoring and digital consultations specifically affect medication adherence and self-management behaviors, lacks direct empirical findings, as the relevant notes provide only study protocols, objectives, or abstracts that cut off before reporting results.
- Sub-question 4, regarding the cost-effectiveness of telemedicine programs compared to traditional in-person clinical care for managing type 2 diabetes, is insufficiently answered by direct comparative data, with current literature noting that evidence regarding cost and economic evaluation remains scarce, limited, or lacking specific trial results for type 2 diabetes.

## Limitations of this search

- Relevance was judged from title and abstract only; 29 of 65 retrieved papers were excluded (score below 3/5 or removed at review 2). Reasons are listed above.
- Summaries, themes and gaps were generated by gemini-3.5-flash-lite from abstracts only and should be checked against the full texts before being relied on.
- Sources searched for every sub-question (20 results per query per source): openalex: 4 of 4 searches answered, 80 records; semanticscholar: 0 of 4 searches answered, 0 records. No full-text access; no citation-chasing or date filtering.
- Automatic failover was used: semanticscholar failed for 4 search(es) and those searches relied on the remaining source(s). Coverage for the affected sub-questions may be narrower.
