## Evidence notes (one per approved paper, from its abstract)

- [1] Cutting Food Waste through Cooperation along the Food Supply Chain (2015) - This study analyzes 44 qualitative expert interviews examining the German food chain to identify the causes and effects of food waste and find methods to reduce it. The results indicate that food waste occurs at all stages, reasons differ between product groups, and communication and shared responsibility among all stakeholders are necessary. Regarding the sub-question, the paper highlights that causes of food waste are found across the value chain, emphasizing the need for stakeholders to share information and work together rather than focusing specifically on supermarket inventory practices.
- [2] Reducing Food Waste at Retail Stores—An Explorative Study (2022) - This explorative study investigates options for how grocery retailers can proactively reduce food waste via better planning of store operations, addressing the trade-off between high product availability and the impacts of overstock. Based on seven case companies focusing on ultra-fres[h products], it examines how retailers manage overstock reactively and explores proactive operational planning. This bears on the sub-question by addressing store operations and overstock management, which are key components of supermarket inventory management practices.
- [3] Quantifying the potential to improve on food waste, freshness and sales for perishables in supermarkets (2017) - This paper introduces two concepts, the Fresh Case Cover and the Efficient Frontier, to improve the performance of fresh departments in supermarkets by reducing waste, increasing freshness, and/or increasing sales, applying them to empirical data from 3 retailers across 27 stores. A regression analysis shows that the Fresh Case Cover, defined as the case pack size divided by the average demand during store shelf life, explains 42% of the variation in waste. ## Themes across the evidence

- Inventory management practices such as optimizing store operations, managing overstock trade-offs, adjusting case pack size relative to demand, and implementing replenishment and discounting policies significantly reduce retail food waste. [2, 3, 4, 5]
- Dynamic pricing, markdown strategies, and expiration-based pricing help stimulate demand for excess or close-to-expiration products, thereby mitigating surplus food generation and waste. [4, 7, 8, 9]
- Active and intelligent packaging technologies, including antimicrobial films, oxygen scavengers, and real-time freshness sensors, help extend product shelf life, monitor quality, and minimize food waste. [17, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 27, 29, 30, 32]
- Consumer-facing packaging design elements, such as appropriate package sizing and ease of emptying, directly influence food waste outcomes at the retail-consumer interface. [28]

## Gaps

- Sub-question 3 regarding how supply chain collaboration and supplier agreements mitigate retail food waste is largely unaddressed by direct empirical studies in the notes, with most papers focusing instead on general supply chain issues, multi-tier sustainability compliance, buyer-supplier friction as a waste generator, or broad conceptual frameworks rather than actionable collaborative mitigation mechanisms.
- The notes lack sufficient empirical evidence regarding direct retail interventions involving formal supplier agreements dedicated specifically to waste reduction.
- There is limited direct evidence concerning the integration of consumer-facing retail innovations other than packaging (such as in-store digital display systems or specific consumer education setups) in minimizing food waste.

## Limitations of this search

- The run was given the constraint 'empirical studies only, not reviews'. It was written into the planning and query-rewriting prompts; it was not applied as a filter to the papers found.
- Queries were reformulated once after a threshold miss for sub-question(s) 2, 3; no further automatic retries were made.
- Sub-question(s) 2 (4 papers) ended with fewer than 5 approved papers; conclusions there rest on thin evidence.
- Relevance was judged from title and abstract only; 72 of 104 retrieved papers were excluded (score below 3/5 or removed at review 2). Reasons are listed above.
- Summaries, themes and gaps were generated by gemini-3.5-flash-lite from abstracts only and should be checked against the full texts before being relied on.
- Sources searched for every sub-question (20 results per query per source): openalex: 6 of 6 searches answered, 120 records; semanticscholar: 0 of 6 searches answered, 0 records. No full-text access; no citation-chasing or date filtering.
- Automatic failover was used: semanticscholar failed for 6 search(es) and those searches relied on the remaining source(s). Coverage for the affected sub-questions may be narrower.
